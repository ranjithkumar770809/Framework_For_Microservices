Architecture Overview

2-Microservice Architecture with Database-per-Service Pattern

Designed for high autonomy, resilience, and loose coupling. Each service is fully self-contained with its own private database and communicates over lightweight HTTP REST.

Distributed Consistency & Compensation

1
User Places Order

Client submits items with quantity to Order Service (Port 8082).

2
Synchronous Stock Check & Deduction

Order Service invokes Product Service (Port 8081) via RestTemplate to check and decrement stock in product_db.

3
Order Persistence

Generates unique Order #, calculates total price, and saves Order & Items to order_db.

4
Compensating Restock on Cancel

If an order is cancelled, Order Service calls PUT /api/products/{id}/restore-stock to automatically restore inventory.

Viva & Exam Defense Guide

Q: Why Database-per-Service instead of a Shared Database?
Ensures domain encapsulation, independent scaling, and fault isolation. If order volume spikes and locks tables in order_db, browsing products in product_db remains completely unaffected.
Q: How is data consistency maintained without cross-database foreign keys?
Logical IDs (e.g. productId) are stored in order_items. Business consistency is enforced programmatically via synchronous REST validation and compensating transactions (reversing stock on failure or cancellation).
